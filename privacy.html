Privacy

RoWhether works without an account.

What we count

We count how many times the app is opened at each location each day, and whether it was opened from the home screen. The count has no address, device or account attached, and it is deleted after three months.

What this browser keeps

RoWhether saves a few things in this browser: each location's settings, the last forecast for up to 10 locations, the locations you opened recently, and your choices for Plan a row and the install bar. They stay on this device and are never sent to RoWhether.

Other services

Your browser asks other services for data. Forecasts and air quality come from Open-Meteo, alerts and observations from the National Weather Service, and river and tide data from USGS and NOAA. When you search for a town, the text you type goes to Open-Meteo's place search. Near me uses your position once, in the browser, and it is not stored or sent. Each service sees your IP address, as any website does.

Suggestions

If you send a suggestion, RoWhether passes your text to Claude, which rewrites it as an issue on the app's public GitHub page. A bug report also includes the app version, your screen size, your browser and your settings. This is the one case where settings leave your device, and only when you send it.
